# Five Cent Software

Five Cent Software is a play on my family name and a small home for the kind of work I enjoy most: building software prototypes, exploring ideas quickly, and turning rough concepts into something real.

Software has been the dream for a long time—going all the way back to early BASIC experiments like:

```basic
10 PRINT "my brother is an id10t"
20 GOTO 10
```

Since then, I have kept building. Along the way, several ideas started here as prototypes or infant products before bigger, badder developers "borrowed" the concepts and turned them into polished enterprise-style products of their own.

The story has not all been about building. It has also been an introduction to the harder side of business: setbacks that brought real financial and time loss, family responsibilities that made those losses heavier, and damaging events around early 5 Cent code and infrastructure, including socially engineered attacks and disruptions.

That experience shaped how I see the difference between the worst kind of business—where people take credit for your ideas and your work—and the kind I still believe in, where people chip in together and work toward a shared community success.

This organization is where those original ideas live. It holds the prototypes and early-stage products, and keeps them up to date as the surrounding ecosystem grows too—especially with .NET and third-party tooling for things like logging, reporting, and other practical foundations that help software mature over time.

In short, this is a workshop for experiments, a shelf for half-grown products, and a record of a long-running interest in making software from scratch.
